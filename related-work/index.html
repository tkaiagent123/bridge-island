    Related work¶

    Policy and oversight context¶

    Modular open systems approaches are required for many U.S. defense programs by statute, and recent oversight work reports on how that has gone. These sources describe the policy setting. They do not describe this repository, and this repository does not implement any program's approach.

    • 10 U.S.C. § 4401, "Requirement for modular open system approach in major defense acquisition programs; definitions". https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/4401
    • U.S. Government Accountability Office, Weapon Systems Acquisition: DOD Needs Better Planning to Attain Benefits of Modular Open Systems, GAO-25-106931, January 22, 2025. https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-25-106931
    • Under Secretary of War for Acquisition and Sustainment, memorandum, "Establishment of Department-Wide Modular Open Systems Approach Policy and Minimum Expectations," September 21,
    • The Department-wide MOSA policy; it directs that earlier conflicting MOSA guidance be rescinded. No Department-hosted copy had been found when this was written. The Department's acquisition office announced it at https://www.linkedin.com/posts/asd-acquisition_establishment-dow-mosa-policy-and-minimum-activity-7524214372768972800-F4hS, and the quotation here was checked against a copy of the memorandum. It says that "Where interfaces differ, efficient translators, adapters, mappings, or other objective integration approaches may be more appropriate than forced re-conformance", which is the role the Translator Module plays here. It also asks that the delivered repository package identify "versions, tailoring, mappings, adapters, extensions, and deviations"; the YAML mapping spec records mappings in a machine-readable, versioned form.

    Background on modularity and mediation¶

    • D. L. Parnas, "On the criteria to be used in decomposing systems into modules," Communications of the ACM, vol. 15, no. 12, pp. 1053-1058, December 1972. doi:10.1145/361598.361623. Decomposing around design decisions that are likely to change, not around processing steps. The Bridge applies this to the baseline's interface: the part that must follow the baseline is isolated from everything else.
    • C. Y. Baldwin and K. B. Clark, Design Rules, Volume 1: The Power of Modularity. MIT Press, 2000. Design rules and hidden modules: independent teams can change what is hidden behind a stable interface.
    • G. Wiederhold, "Mediators in the architecture of future information systems," IEEE Computer, vol. 25, no. 3, pp. 38-49, March 1992. doi:10.1109/2.121508. A mediator is a module between data resources and their users that holds the knowledge needed to bridge their differences. The Bridge is a mediator in this sense. (Some reference lists spell the title "Mediation in the Architecture of Future Information Systems".)
    • M. E. Conway, "How do committees invent?," Datamation, vol. 14, no. 4, pp. 28-31, April 1968. Systems tend to mirror the communication structure of the organizations that build them.

    Related patterns, measures and standards¶

    These were checked against their public pages. No claim is made that this project implements or conforms to any standard listed here.

    • The anti-corruption layer. The pattern places a layer between subsystems that do not share semantics, so that one can stay unchanged without compromising the design of the other. It was first described by Eric Evans in Domain-Driven Design: Tackling Complexity in the Heart of Software. "Anti-Corruption Layer Pattern", Azure Architecture Center, Microsoft Learn. https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/architecture/patterns/anti-corruption-layer. The Bridge is the closest industry analogue, and the same page lists the costs this project documents: added latency, an extra component to operate, enforcing input validation at a boundary between different trust levels, observability to diagnose translation failures, and keeping business rules out of the layer. It also says the pattern may not suit systems without significant semantic differences, which matches the limits stated here.
    • Age of information. S. Kaul, R. Yates and M. Gruteser, "Real-time status: How often should one update?," in Proc. IEEE INFOCOM, 2012, pp. 2731-2735. Introduced the age of information as a measure of freshness. It is why this project reports the average and peak age over time, not only the age at arrival.
    • Valid time and transaction time. C. S. Jensen and C. E. Dyreson (eds.), "The Consensus Glossary of Temporal Database Concepts - February 1998 Version." The vocabulary for what G3 does: the source time is the valid time of a fact, and the delivery time is the time the system learned of it. https://infolab.usc.edu/csci599/Fall2001/paper/glossary.pdf (a copy of the glossary).
    • Design structure and propagation cost. A. MacCormack, J. Rusnak and C. Y. Baldwin, "Exploring the structure of complex software designs: An empirical study of open source and proprietary code," Management Science, vol. 52, no. 7, pp. 1015-1030, 2006. doi:10.1287/mnsc.1060.0552. Uses design structure matrices of element-to-element dependencies, as the dependency edges counted in the experiments are, and defines a propagation-cost measure of how far a change can spread. This project counts one-step dependencies; it does not compute propagation cost.
    • Medical device integration. ASTM F2761-09(2013), Medical Devices and Medical Systems - Essential safety requirements for equipment comprising the patient-centric integrated clinical environment (ICE) - Part 1: General requirements and conceptual model. A standard about integrating equipment from different manufacturers into one patient-centric system. The medical example is a structural illustration only and makes no clinical claim.
    • A published open avionics architecture. The FACE Approach, The Open Group. It defines a Reference Architecture, which includes a Transport Services Segment, and a Data Architecture. https://www.opengroup.org/face. It separates how data is moved from how it is represented, as this project separates the native-protocol ports from the Translator Module.

    Used by the insulin-loop example¶

    None of these documents endorses anything here, and the example claims no compliance with any of them.

    • The minimal model. R. N. Bergman, Y. Z. Ider, C. R. Bowden and C. Cobelli, "Quantitative estimation of insulin sensitivity," Am J Physiol 236(6): E667-E677, 1979. doi:10.1152/ajpendo.1979.236.6.E667. The structure of the invented patient model.
    • Time in range. T. Battelino et al., "Clinical Targets for Continuous Glucose Monitoring Data Interpretation: Recommendations From the International Consensus on Time in Range," Diabetes Care,
    • PMID 31177185. The source of the 70-180, below 70, below 54, above 180 and above 250 mg/dL thresholds used in the results.
    • Medical application platforms. J. Hatcliff, A. King, I. Lee, A. MacDonald, A. Fernando, M. Robkin, E. Vasserman, S. Weininger and J. M. Goldman, "Rationale and Architecture Principles for Medical Application Platforms," in Proc. ACM/IEEE ICCPS, 2012. doi:10.1109/ICCPS.2012.9. It argues for a platform that mediates between independently made devices and the apps that use them. That is the role the Bridge plays here.
    • Regulation (cited by title, for the traceability matrix):
      • 21 CFR 862.1356, Interoperable automated glycemic controller;
      • FDA, Multiple Function Device Products: Policy and Considerations (July 2020);
      • FDA, Predetermined Change Control Plans for Medical Devices (draft guidance, August 22, 2024);
      • IEC 80001-1:2021, on the application of risk management to IT networks that incorporate medical devices.

    Open mission-system standards (U.S. Air Force)¶

    The U.S. Air Force publishes unclassified mission-system standards at https://open-arsenal.gitlab.io/, with repositories on GitHub and GitLab. Three of them describe an element or pattern at the same boundary the Bridge occupies. These were checked against the public files. No claim is made that this project implements or conforms to them.

    • U.S. Air Force, OMS Standard, OMSC-STD-001 Rev M, OMS v2.5, January 22, 2026. Defines the Isolator as an "isolation layer between Mission Package and an external system", and gives the aim of constraining the architecture to "bound/limit where and to what degree re-certification would be required upon change" (sections 3.4.6, Isolation, and 6.4, Isolators). https://github.com/open-arsenal/oms
    • U.S. Air Force, A-GRA Vehicle Interface Volume, ASK 6.0a, July 31, 2026. Routes all communications from the mission package to a safety-critical enclave through a VI OMS Isolator, at what it calls the "Airworthiness Boundary", and assumes the Isolator contains a message validator and a platform adapter (section 1.1). https://github.com/open-arsenal/a-gra
    • AFLCMC/WA, AMS GRA Architecture Volumes, Executive Summary and Document Catalog, Version 14.0, April 24, 2026. Says that interactions crossing its boundary may use "adaptation and isolation patterns" so that architectural updates do not trigger platform recertification (section 2.1). Its Compliance Assessment Approach assesses a legacy element and its adapters together as one unit. https://github.com/open-arsenal/ams-gra

    Where they differ¶

    • Both sides specified. The Isolator's Service Contract covers its OMS-facing side, and OMS says that exchanges facing the external elements "do not need to be documented in OMS documentation" (6.4). Here the mapping spec and the OIU and UID describe both sides, including a baseline that adopts no common standard.
    • Assumed standard. OMS requires the Isolator to use OMS messages over the Platform's service bus through the Critical Abstraction Layer on its OMS-facing side (STD-000431). The Bridge assumes no shared standard on either side; translating between two native interfaces is its whole job.
    • The re-certification bound. The sections cited here, and the Isolator Checklist Instructions (OMSC-INS-012), state the aim and say an Isolator may need certification, but do not specify a method for establishing the bound. They leave it to the adopting program. This project makes the extent of a change countable (guidelines G1 to G6 and the change-impact experiment), on invented numbers and without any claim about certification.

    A Bridge whose Unique-Island side presents an OMS-facing interface would be, in OMS terms, an Isolator, so the two can be used together and do not compete. Concepts defines the Bridge's parts.
