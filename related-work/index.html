    Related work¶

    Policy and oversight context¶

    Modular open systems approaches are required for many U.S. defense programs by statute, and recent oversight work reports on how that has gone. These sources describe the policy setting. They do not describe this repository, and this repository does not implement any program's approach.

    • 10 U.S.C. § 4401, "Requirement for modular open system approach in major defense acquisition programs; definitions". https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/4401
    • U.S. Government Accountability Office, Weapon Systems Acquisition: DOD Needs Better Planning to Attain Benefits of Modular Open Systems, GAO-25-106931, January 22, 2025. https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-25-106931

    Background on modularity and mediation¶

    • D. L. Parnas, "On the criteria to be used in decomposing systems into modules," Communications of the ACM, vol. 15, no. 12, pp. 1053-1058, December 1972. doi:10.1145/361598.361623. Decomposing around design decisions that are likely to change, not around processing steps. The Bridge applies this to the baseline's interface: the part that must follow the baseline is isolated from everything else.
    • C. Y. Baldwin and K. B. Clark, Design Rules, Volume 1: The Power of Modularity. MIT Press, 2000. Design rules and hidden modules: independent teams can change what is hidden behind a stable interface.
    • G. Wiederhold, "Mediators in the architecture of future information systems," IEEE Computer, vol. 25, no. 3, pp. 38-49, March 1992. doi:10.1109/2.121508. A mediator is a module between data resources and their users that holds the knowledge needed to bridge their differences. The Bridge is a mediator in this sense. (Some reference lists spell the title "Mediation in the Architecture of Future Information Systems".)
    • M. E. Conway, "How do committees invent?," Datamation, vol. 14, no. 4, pp. 28-31, April 1968. Systems tend to mirror the communication structure of the organizations that build them.

    Related patterns, measures and standards¶

    These were checked against their public pages. No claim is made that this project implements or conforms to any standard listed here.

    • The anti-corruption layer. The pattern places a layer between subsystems that do not share semantics, so that one can stay unchanged without compromising the design of the other. It was first described by Eric Evans in Domain-Driven Design: Tackling Complexity in the Heart of Software. "Anti-Corruption Layer Pattern", Azure Architecture Center, Microsoft Learn. https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/architecture/patterns/anti-corruption-layer. The Bridge is the closest industry analogue, and the same page lists the costs this project documents: added latency, an extra component to operate, enforcing input validation at a boundary between different trust levels, observability to diagnose translation failures, and keeping business rules out of the layer. It also says the pattern may not suit systems without significant semantic differences, which matches the limits stated here.
    • Age of information. S. Kaul, R. Yates and M. Gruteser, "Real-time status: How often should one update?," in Proc. IEEE INFOCOM, 2012, pp. 2731-2735. Introduced the age of information as a measure of freshness. It is why this project reports the average and peak age over time, not only the age at arrival.
    • Valid time and transaction time. C. S. Jensen and C. E. Dyreson (eds.), "The Consensus Glossary of Temporal Database Concepts - February 1998 Version." The vocabulary for what G3 does: the source time is the valid time of a fact, and the delivery time is the time the system learned of it. https://infolab.usc.edu/csci599/Fall2001/paper/glossary.pdf (a copy of the glossary).
    • Design structure and propagation cost. A. MacCormack, J. Rusnak and C. Y. Baldwin, "Exploring the structure of complex software designs: An empirical study of open source and proprietary code," Management Science, vol. 52, no. 7, pp. 1015-1030, 2006. doi:10.1287/mnsc.1060.0552. Uses design structure matrices of element-to-element dependencies, as the dependency edges counted in the experiments are, and defines a propagation-cost measure of how far a change can spread. This project counts one-step dependencies; it does not compute propagation cost.
    • Medical device integration. ASTM F2761-09(2013), Medical Devices and Medical Systems - Essential safety requirements for equipment comprising the patient-centric integrated clinical environment (ICE) - Part 1: General requirements and conceptual model. A standard about integrating equipment from different manufacturers into one patient-centric system. The medical example is a structural illustration only and makes no clinical claim.
    • A published open avionics architecture. The FACE Approach, The Open Group. It defines a Reference Architecture, which includes a Transport Services Segment, and a Data Architecture. https://www.opengroup.org/face. It separates how data is moved from how it is represented, as this project separates the native-protocol ports from the Translator Module.
